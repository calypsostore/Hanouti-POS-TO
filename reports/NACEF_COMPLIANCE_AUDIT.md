# Tunisia NACEF Fiscal Cash-Register Compliance Audit

- **Audit date:** 2026-11-25
- **Project reviewed:** Hanouti POS, repository source and local Supabase migrations
- **Scope:** Technical readiness for on-premises food and beverage businesses under Tunisia's fiscal cash-register framework.

**Conclusion:** Hanouti POS is **not demonstrated to be NACEF-compliant or homologated**. This is a source/design audit, not a legal opinion, certification assessment, or inspection of the deployed hosted database.

## 1. Executive summary

The project has a useful POS and fiscal-calculation foundation: server-validated checkout, store-scoped order records, integer-millime calculations, versioned tax rules, transaction snapshots, void workflows, activity logs, shift reports, and a browser offline queue. Those components can inform a future certified cash-register application.

The legal system described in the primary sources is materially more than a POS with VAT reports. It requires a certified cash register (CE) with a protected fiscal-data module (MDF), controlled registration and certificates, communication with the Ministry's NACEF platform, prescribed signed/verified tickets, traceability of software and operating systems, and daily electronic reports. No NACEF/MDF adapter, fiscal signing flow, NACEF ticket QR, fiscal-device identity, device enrollment, or NACEF transmission workflow was found in the application or local migrations.

**The product must not be presented as a NACEF cash register, certified fiscal device, NACEF-integrated system, or legally compliant receipt system on the evidence reviewed.** No provider accreditation, equipment/software homologation certificate, device certificate, or Ministry registration document was found in the repository. Those external facts are therefore not verifiable here.

One article reference in the task brief needs correction based on the primary legal text: **Decree No. 2019-1126 Article 7 requires two electronic daily reports; Article 8 lists the receipt/ticket information.** The relevant issue is JORT No. 99 of 10 December 2019. The 14 October 2025 Minister of Finance decision is in JORT No. 125, page 2664.

## 3. Legal applicability and implementation deadlines

Decree Article 2 requires natural and legal persons providing on-premises consumption services to use the transaction-register system for customer transactions. Article 18 assigns the Minister of Finance responsibility for determining classification criteria and compliance dates.

Article 1 of the 14 October 2025 decision defines the covered activity as the sale, as a primary or secondary activity, of prepared or ready-to-consume food and/or beverages together with on-premises consumption service. A store's actual coverage cannot be determined from its POS category alone; the taxpayer's legal form, activity and official classification need confirmation.

The decision's Article 2 sets the following start dates:

| Covered category | Compliance date stated in the decision | Position on audit date |
|---|---:|---|
| Legal persons operating tourist-classified restaurants, salons de thé, and second- or third-category cafés | 1 November 2025 | Date has passed |
| Other legal persons providing the covered on-premises services | 1 July 2026 | Date has passed |
| Natural persons under the real tax regime and required to file monthly tax returns, providing the covered services | 1 July 2027 | Future date |
| Other natural persons providing the covered services | 1 July 2028 | Future date |

These are the dates in the reviewed 2025 decision, not a determination that a particular Hanouti customer belongs to a category. The decree's Article 17 refers to sanctions under Article 94 of the Tax Rights and Procedures Code. A Tunisian tax professional or the competent Ministry office should confirm the taxpayer's classification, any later amendments, and the legal effect for the specific business.

## 5. Existing features that can be reused

- **POS and order workflow:** store-specific catalog, staff identity, shifts, sales, payment-method classification, idempotency, and server-side order creation are a solid operational base ([PosScreen.tsx](../src/screens/PosScreen.tsx), [orderService.ts](../src/services/orderService.ts), [order workflows](../supabase/migrations/20261119121500_order_workflows.sql)).
- **Fiscal calculation foundation:** dated tax rules and item/category treatments feed a versioned per-line snapshot; TypeScript and PostgreSQL use integer millimes, and the server recalculates/compares checkout data ([taxEngine.ts](../src/services/fiscal/taxEngine.ts), [fiscal_checkout migration](../supabase/migrations/20261125120000_fiscal_checkout.sql)). It can be retained as a calculation input/component if the approved MDF interface and homologation tests accept it.
- **Snapshot integrity:** order snapshots are checked for consistency and have a database trigger against snapshot-field updates. Tax rules, activity logs, and reports have append-only protections. These are useful controls, but are not a signed fiscal ledger.
- **Void and audit workflows:** void requests, reasons, owner/PIN resolution, actor attribution, store activity logs, and the internal append-only order event history provide a basis for a future correction workflow ([order transaction event history migration](../supabase/migrations/20261125230000_order_transaction_event_history.sql)). They must be adapted to emit the official corrective fiscal event rather than treating status changes as a refund. The internal history is not an official or signed fiscal ledger, and pre-migration sales are intentionally not backfilled.
- **Shift close and reports:** current X/Z workflows reconcile local sales, voids, payments, and counts. They can support operational management alongside—not in place of—the official Article 7 outputs.
- **Tenant isolation:** store IDs and authenticated RLS policies are a reusable SaaS boundary. A separate fiscal-device identity and register-level authorization model is still required.
- **Offline UX:** the queue and attention states can be retained for non-fiscal work or adapted only if the approved MDF supports the exact offline fiscal mode. Browser IndexedDB must not be treated as the official fiscal module.

## 6. Missing technical components

1. An approved CE-to-MDF adapter and the exact supported S-MDF/E-MDF version.
2. Per-register identity, serial/device registration, IMDF, certificate enrollment, renewal/revocation, and terminal mapping.
3. Fiscal operation types for sale, refund/correction, pro-forma and training, with signed relationships to original transactions.
4. A compliant operation state machine that does not make an original fiscal transaction disappear or mutate.
5. MDF-generated signature, QR, ticket identifiers, final numbering, and platform response/acknowledgement.
6. The complete Article 8 ticket content, including cashier/device identifiers, operation/ticket type, tax lines, payment amount and change.
7. Official NACEF daily electronic close and daily financial report generation and submission.
8. Approved offline signing/sequence/synchronization plus outage reporting and maintenance-limit tracking.
9. Per-device software/OS version traceability, tamper/suspicious-intervention alerts, and provider maintenance logs.
10. A documented, tested homologation release process binding the exact CE/MDF/software/OS versions and installed equipment.

## 7. Required hardware, software, provider, and approval evidence

Before any live claim or production use, obtain and retain:

- Evidence that the supplier is accredited by the competent Ministry services and that the exact CE/system and MDF versions are homologated.
- An approved E-MDF device or the Ministry-provided S-MDF client/agent deployment applicable to the chosen terminal/server arrangement. The public NACEF FAQ describes both options; it does not certify this application.
- A Ministry-issued electronic certificate for each register and successful pre-use registration/enrollment for each device.
- Provider-confirmed device serial/manufacturing details, required network configuration (the public FAQ mentions the NACEF-specific APN in the certificate workflow), and the taxpayer/device association.
- The current technical and functional specification, test-environment instructions, supported error/state behavior, and written release/change-control obligations.
- Provider maintenance/support arrangement. Decree Art. 15 sets immediate fault notification and repair timelines; the accredited provider is the required repair channel.
- Homologation/test reports and a decision/certificate covering the exact deployed software, MDF, hardware, and version. A successful test suite or a generic supplier statement is not homologation evidence.

## 8. Integration architecture proposal

This is a proposal for discussion with an accredited provider; it deliberately does not define undocumented API calls or cryptography.

1. **Keep Hanouti as the user-facing CE layer.** Preserve catalog, ordering, staff workflow, and store isolation where they pass provider review. Add a narrow fiscal-device adapter boundary instead of embedding guessed NACEF behavior in screens.
2. **Create a canonical immutable transaction request.** Include the approved operation type, register and cashier identities, line quantities/prices, tax inputs, payment/tender/change, transaction time, and idempotency reference required by the provider specification.
3. **Delegate fiscal authority to the approved MDF.** The approved module must supply the required fiscal record/signature/QR and NACEF status. Do not treat a locally calculated snapshot, locally generated QR, ordinary order ID, or Supabase write as a signed fiscal event.
4. **Persist fiscal evidence append-only.** Store the accepted MDF/NACEF reference, signature/QR payload or permitted reference, operation type, original/correction linkage, platform state, and immutable request/response evidence only in the form allowed by the specification. Keep operational order data separate from the official fiscal ledger.
5. **Represent corrections as new fiscal events.** Implement refund, cancellation/correction, pro-forma and training as distinct supported operations. Preserve the original transaction and link every compensating event as prescribed.
6. **Use MDF-owned offline behavior.** Route offline queuing, signing, numbering and synchronization through the provider-approved module. Add incident reporting and recovery UX; do not upgrade the current browser queue to “NACEF offline” by labeling alone.
7. **Generate official daily reports from the certified fiscal source.** Keep X/Z management reports, but render and archive the Article 7 outputs separately from the authorized MDF/NACEF data and acknowledgements.
8. **Gate every release and terminal.** Tie build/version, OS/device information, provider maintenance, certificate status, and homologation evidence to each deployed register; reject unsupported or expired states according to provider requirements.

## Final assessment

The existing POS can provide reusable front-end and operational components, and its fiscal snapshots/reporting can support internal reconciliation. It is missing the central certified fiscal components and workflows that distinguish a NACEF CEF from a conventional POS. Eligibility, provider accreditation, certificates, device registration, protocol compatibility, and homologation remain unverified and require documentary confirmation from the Ministry/accredited provider. **No source-code finding in this audit is evidence of official compliance or homologation.**
